SOCIETAL VIEWPOINT ON PHONES

Since the iPhone's launch, smartphones have brought us many benefits such as better communication, while older mobile phones gave us that same ability it was sort of limited since you could only communicate with the contacts you had but when you compare it with today, yes it works the same but people have way more options for communication, apps like Instagram, snapchat, discord have allowed us to connect and make friends with people all across the globe. Connectivity is also something smartphones have greatly improved since friends and family can text, call, video chat, and share photos. A point that almost no one ever mentions when it comes to phones is how they have reduced isolation, many online discussions and articles like to argue the opposite and while they are right they only focus on the negative aspects and never consider the positives, many people have made connections through online communities such as games or forums bonding through shared interests with around 33% to 35% of young adults and teenagers meeting their online friends in real life, face-to-face according to articles from RTE and BBC. Something positive that unfortunately comes with many negatives. 

Studies from Oregon State University have addressed questions such as "Do online-only connections count" and "Can these virtual relationships do for us what real-life relationships do?" with the cruel answer being no and are said to even make things worse. Whenever we go on social media apps such as Instagram we get bombarded with photos of the lives of complete strangers, causing many to try compare themselves with said strangers seeing what they have that we lack, this has caused many feelings of inadequacy in others with them constantly feeling the need to compare themselves, not seeing how it affects their mental health and causes depression. What these individuals fail to understand is how these photos, these videos and stories that we see online are carefully curated to highlight the best parts of our lives, they look good on purpose because a majority of people want to appear perfect, or exciting, the viewers don't understand how to separate this photos from reality and realize they can't judge someone based of random photos cause again these photos are made to highlight the best parts of our lives not everything we ever do. The real reason though for why people who interact online feel lonely is not solely due these pictures you see on Instagram but the lack of meaningful connections that online interactions provide us with. Online connections lack depth and emotional connections, they lack the nuances of nonverbal cues which makes it very difficult to understand the meaning and emotions behind a message, resulting in misunderstanding and disconnections. This absence of emotional bonds and superficial connections is what has caused many people to still feel lonely despite having probably hundreds of online friends, it's their need for genuine understanding and a real human connection that causes them to feel this way.

Communication is not the only socila impact smartphones have had, things like research, travel, education and more have also impacted society in big ways, however I want to mainly focus on two points, that being research and education, starting of research. Before smartphones, society had to rely on many different means of research, they would have to rely on books, newspapers, libraries, post, word of mouth and so on, it was quite the hassle due to needing to look through multiple different sources just to find what you were looking for, this all changed of course when the world wide web was invented in 1989 but was limited to the computers of the time, that again changed when the first iPhone was introduced in 2007. With the first iPhone having a built in browser it's safe to say that this revolutionary at the time, society was no longer limited to using computers for access to the world wide web as now they had a miniature tool at their disposal that was significantly cheaper than any laptop or computer in 2007. I'd like to consider the world wide web a massive digital library that people can access at any time, this however has caused a few problems. Because facts are now one quick search away this has caused an affect on human retention, a phenomenon called "Brain drain", which refers to the reduction in focus and cognitive ability since why try to remember something or use the brain to figure it out when your phone is right there and the answer can be easily looked up, this has created compulsive checking loops since people have developed attention struggles due to lack of focus. We also have to consider the fact that even with this vast amount of information at our disposal, much of it can be misinformative, fake news is very common occurrence with it flooding social media apps like Facebook and Instagram, even official news sites such as CNN and The New York Times have had cases where they needed to revise over articles because they were misinformative, the dangers of fake news is very under looked as it's impacted some people in very harmful ways with their trust being broken, leading to them losing confidence in journalism or science which has left them vulnerable to manipulation by trusting the wrong sources.

The second point I'd like to focus on is education. Education is perhaps the most important topic to discuss when it comes to how smartphones, or technology in general, has affected the world as with all the changes they've brought it has heavily impacted how people learn, with many groups having differing viewpoints on whether it's impacted us negatively or positively. To start off let's discuss why most schools are against the use of smartphones, cyberbullying or anything related to mental health is a massive concern for any school as students have a tendency to start drama in one or other, whether that be escalating tiny issues or spreading around big ones and it doesn't take for long to spread either thanks to the likes of social media connecting everybody, situations like this has lead to many cases of cyberbullying and has taken tolls on student's mental health and in even extreme cases might've ruined friendships and relationships based on who is involved in the drama. Shari Obrenski, president of the Cleveland Teachers Union says that students like to use smartphones to schedule, record, and post fights to social media, with a quote, “They were using the phones to create chaos and perpetuate violence in our schools.”, and from my personal experience this has definitely been the case and I'm sure many other would agree with me. With issues like these arising it's become harder for teachers to contain and manage student aggression, their solutions to try combat this issue like restricting phone access to school WIFI or banning smartphones altogether have proved to be ineffective due to reasons like students using private channels like Snap Chat or Instagram, or simply not complying with school rules. An argument for why some schools are for smartphones is that banning smartphones altogether will prevent vital communication for students in the case of an emergency, take for example a panic attack, or someone faints, or there's a fire, or someone has a medical emergency and so on, students should be allowed away to contact their parents or emergency services if needed to, a large portion of parents believe that if adults are allowed to leave with their phones then so should teens and children, why restrict something that could end up helping many students and even parents. On the other hand though schools believe that a ban or a limited use of smartphones will help minimize distractions for both students and teachers, the "brain drain" effect states that the mere presence of smartphone takes up mental space which ends up leaving students with a lower capacity and analytic ability for other tasks, even when focused the mere effort to try avoid checking the phone reduces both available working memory capacity and functional fluid intelligence. By restricting when and where students are allowed to use their phones will help reduce that temptation to check your phone and give young students a break from all the constant pings and notifications that litter their phones, both school and researchers believe that this will help in reducing brain drain. Another argument that is for the use of smartphones in schools is that it helps students learn in a way that best suits them, to put it simply school systems can be very rigid and one size doesn't fit all, one person might prefer to learn by reading notes while another may prefer to learn through the use of flashcards or videos online, and when it comes to studying how students learn is very divisive, sure you could just study at home but what about when you have study classes and your school prohibits the use of smartphones or iPads, how is a student meant to study in a way that both suits and benefits them when their options are limited. To summarise, while there are very important issues that come with using smartphones in schools they are not all negative and the use of smartphones in schools can also be very beneficial for students, the main problem is trying to manage their use and what they're being used for.
