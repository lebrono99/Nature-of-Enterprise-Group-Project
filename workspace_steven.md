SOCIETAL VIEWPOINT ON PHONES

Since their creation, mobile phones slowly started to integrate into society and now with smartphones in the modern day it is at a point where the availability of smartphones are now so common and a basic resource that everyone has, even as early as the age of 9 it is common for you to already own a smartphone. Societal views on phones have changed a lot in such short amounts of time, before mobile phones became such a basic resource for people to use, they were stereotypically seen as something only used by businesspeople which makes sense for the time, mobile phones didn't have the multitude of functions current-day smartphones are built with before they were only used for communication, businesspeople would use them to contact clients and colleagues, but the most significant reason for why old mobile phones were associated with businesspeople was because they were the only ones able to afford them. Like many other technologies at the time, mobile phones were considered an advanced piece of technology and due to their low production volumes and expensive manufacturing only the wealthy were able to afford mobile phones. It was only until the second generation of mobile phones did societal viewpoints change, as mobile phones finally became available for the wide populous to afford.

The second generation of mobile phones were significantly cheaper than the first and with them having the ability to text for the first time they became very popular with teenagers, beginning the transition to mobile phones becoming a common resource with Nokia being a key player for phones slowly being integrated into society. While Nokia nowadays is considered a dying company the societal impact it had in the 1990s was massive as it's Nokia that laid the groundwork, they became the world's leading phone brand allowing millions for the first time to communicate and send texts. But while Nokia is the one that led to phones becoming common, it was companies such as apple and the introduction of the first iPhone that truly impacted modern society.

The iPhone's launch in 2007 revolutionized modern day phones as it set the blueprint for other companies like Samsung to follow, it changed the way we communicate, work, and interact with the world as the iPhone is what introduced us to the concept of social media and apps and while it has lead to many positive changes in the world, it also laid the way for many negative impacts. Social media is infamous for being more bad than good, especially for more younger groups as social media has lead to many cases such as stalking, cyberbullying, toxicity, hate speech, scams, hacking, grooming, people developing mental health issues and much more, it's mainly due to social media that these negative viewpoints on how phones impact society have developed, it's lead to arguments on when children should be allowed phones, and it's even lead to problems in the modern day where screen addiction is more prevalent than ever before, as much of out youth are now known to spend the majority of their time with their eyes glued to an ipad.

Since the iPhone's launch, smartphones have brought us many benefits such as better communication, while older mobile phones gave us that same ability it was sort of limited since you could only communicate with the contacts you had but when you compare it with today, yes it works the same but people have way more options for communication, apps like Instagram, snapchat, discord have allowed us to connect and make friends with people all across the globe. Connectivity is also something smartphones have greatly improved since friends and family can text, call, video chat, and share photos. A point that almost no one ever mentions when it comes to phones is how they have reduced isolation, many online discussions and articles like to argue the opposite and while they are right they only focus on the negative aspects and never consider the positives, many people have made connections through online communities such as games or forums bonding through shared interests with around 33% to 35% of young adults and teenagers meeting their online friends in real life, face-to-face according to articles from RTE and BBC. Navigation as well has been made more convenient along with accessibility to emergency services, access to multiple entertainment services and so on. But for every positive there is a negative.

Earlier I mentioned how smartphones have given individuals the ability to make friends online, something very good that unfortunately also comes with negatives, studies from Oregon State University have addressed questions such as "Do online-only connections count" and "Can these virtual relationships do for us what real-life relationships do?" with the cruel answer being no and are said to even make things worse. Whenever we go on social media apps such as Instagram we get bombarded with photos of the lives of complete strangers, causing many to try compare themselves with said strangers seeing what they have that we lack, this has caused many feelings of inadequacy in others with them constantly feeling the need to compare themselves, not seeing how it affects their mental health and causes depression. What these individuals fail to understand is how these photos, these videos and stories that we see online are carefully curated to highlight the best parts of our lives, they look good on purpose because a majority of people want to appear perfect, or exciting, or make it seem like their life is so much fun because they're constantly doing something or are always with friends, the viewers don't understand how to separate this photos from reality and realize they can't judge someone based of random photos cause again these photos are made to highlight the best parts of our lives not everything we ever do. The real reason though for why people who interact online feel lonely is not solely due these pictures you see on Instagram but the lack of meaningful connections that online interactions provide us with. Online connections lack depth and emotional connections, they lack the nuances of nonverbal cues which makes it very difficult to understand the meaning and emotions behind a message, resulting in misunderstanding and disconnections. This absence of emotional bonds and superficial connections is what has caused many people to still feel lonely despite having probably hundreds of online friends, it's their need for genuine understanding and a real human connection that causes them to feel this way.

Smartphones have impacted society in more way than one though, it's not just communication and connectivity that has improved but also education, travel, searching for information and more, however I want to mainly focus on my first three points, starting of with finding information. Before smartphones, society had to rely on many different means of searching for information, they would have to rely on books, newspapers, libraries, post, word of mouth and so on, it was quite the hassle due to needing to look through multiple different sources just to find what you were looking for, this all changed of course when the world wide web was invented in 1989 but was limited to the computers of the time, that again changed when the first iPhone was introduced in 2007. With the first iPhone having a built in browser it's safe to say that this revolutionary at the time, society was no longer limited to using computers for access to the world wide web as now they had a miniature tool at their disposal that was significantly cheaper than any laptop or computer in 2007, as well it was also much more convenient since with phones all you had to do was reach from your pocket meanwhile with computers you had to go to either libraries or your home to use them, but even then you would be forced to either wait or share the use of the computer as most homes typically owned one computer and depending on how many people where in your house or needed to use the computer you would be forced to wait an unknown amount of time to be able to use it next, smartphones completely eliminated this inconvenience. I'd like to consider the world wide web a massive digital library that people can access at any time, this however has caused a few problems. Because facts are now one quick search away this has caused an affect on human retention, a phenomenon called "Brain drain", which refers to the reduction in focus and cognitive ability since why try to remember something or use the brain to figure it out when your phone is right there and the answer can be easily looked up, this has created compulsive checking loops since people have developed attention struggles due to lack of focus. We also have to consider the fact that even with this vast amount of information at our disposal, much of it can be misinformative, fake news is very common occurrence with it flooding social media apps like Facebook and Instagram, even official news sites such as CNN and The New York Times have had cases where they needed to revise over articles because they were misinformative, the dangers of fake news is very under looked as it's impacted some people in very harmful ways with their trust being broken, leading to them losing confidence in journalism or science which has left them vulnerable to manipulation by trusting the wrong sources. Fake news has also lead to health risks due to the spread of false claims or dangerous advice during moments of crisis, fake news has also lead to harassment and mob violence plus much more.
