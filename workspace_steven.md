SOCIETAL VIEWPOINT ON PHONES

Since the iPhone's launch, smartphones have brought us many benefits such as better communication and connectivity, apps like Instagram and snapchat have allowed us to connect and make friends with people across the globe, some may argue they have reduced isolation, many people have made connections through online communities such as games or forums bonding through shared interests with around 33% to 35% of young adults and teenagers meeting their online friends in real life, face-to-face according to articles from RTE and BBC. Something positive that unfortunately comes with many negatives. 

Studies from Oregon State University have addressed the question "Can these virtual relationships do for us what real-life relationships do?" with the sad answer being no and are said to even make things worse. The reason for why people who interact online feel lonely is because of the lack of meaningful connections that online interactions provide us with. They lack depth and the nuances of nonverbal cues which makes it very difficult to understand the meaning and emotions behind a message, resulting in misunderstandings. This absence of emotional bonds and is what has caused many people to still feel lonely despite having perhaps hundreds of online friends, it's their need for a real human connection that causes them to feel this way.

Research and education are two things that have also been impacted greatly by the use of smartphones, starting with research. Before smartphones, society had to rely on many different means of research, such as books, newspapers, libraries, etc. It was quite the hassle due to needing to look through multiple different sources just to find an answer, this all changed with the first iPhone being introduced. It’s built-in browser no longer limited society to using computers for access to the world wide web as now they had a miniature tool at their disposal that was significantly cheaper than any computer in 2007. But because facts are now one quick search away this has caused an effect on human retention, a phenomenon called "Brain drain", which refers to the reduction in focus and cognitive ability since why try to remember something or use the brain to figure it out an answer that can be easily looked up, this has caused people to develop attention struggles. 

Moving on to education. Students have a tendency to start drama by escalating tiny issues or spreading around big ones, situations like this has led to cases of cyberbullying and has taken tolls on student's mental health. With issues like these arising it's become harder for teachers to manage student aggression, their solutions to try combat this issue like restricting phone access to school WIFI or banning smartphones altogether have proved to be ineffective due to reasons like students using private channels like Snap Chat, or simply not complying with school rules. An argument that is for the use of smartphones in schools is that it helps students learn in a way that best suits them, to put it simply school systems can be very rigid, one person might prefer to learn by reading notes while another may prefer to learn through the use of videos online, what if there are study classes and your school prohibits the use of smartphones, how is a student meant to study in a way that benefits them when their options are limited. To summarise, while there are very important issues that come with using smartphones in schools they are not all negative and the use of smartphones in schools can also be very beneficial for students, the main problem is trying to manage their use.
