## Exploitation of workers
One ethical problem with mobile phones is where they come from. Parts of mobile phones contain rare earth materials that only come from countries like the Democratic Republic of Congo(DRC). Workers mine for cobalt and are often exploited. For example, a Congolese boy named Paul was exploited to work in the mines for a day straight, using him for child labour. He started mining when he was 12 and worked with little to no safety equipment and little pay. He was constantly ill and sick while underground. This affects his physical health, the harmful dust he breathed while working will affect his health in the long term. Paul was also at risk of the mines collapsing on him, as the mines are dark, tight spaces and are always prone to collapsing. Around 80 miners have died underground during 2014-2015.

There is no coporate shame on the companies buying these materials. The material is transported from the mines to large markets and then bought by companies. The three top buyers for cobalt are Ningbo Shanshan Tianjin Bamo from China and L&F Materials from South Korea, where they reportedly bought $90 million worth of cobalt in 2013. This material is then exported to different countries like China and Korea. Where it is then used to produce batteries for electric devices like smartphones or vehicles like EVs. 

## Environmental impact
Another ethical problem is the carbon footprint of smartphones. In 2022, smartphones produced 146 millions tons of carbon dioxide emissions from manufacturing and shipping. 
The factories where smartphones are manufactured uses a lot of energy, which is mostly made from fossil fuels, increasing greenhouse gas emissions. Most of the smartphone's carbon footprint comes from the manufacturing phase, where it is 70% to 85%
Cobalt has been historically mined for years, meaning that the mining has caused ecological damage to the local environments. The trees and other greenery that was once there cannot take in the carbon and turn into oxygen. The ecological damage has also destroyed natural habitats, meaning that the local wildlife are also displaced.

Throwing away smartphones can cause fatal damage to the ecosystems, plants, animals and people living there. They end up in landfills every year through illegal dumping and other ways, this is known as e-waste. 
Smartphone batteries contain harmful materials like mercury and lead. When it is dumped, it can leak and pollute different water sources which is deadly, as fish can consume this material, infecting it, and be spread to humans through consumption.

## Solutions
To combat the exploitation of workers in Congo, support organisations like Amnesty International, where they fight for exploited workers and human rights. Currently, Amnesty are calling out multinational companies that buy these rare earth materials to investigate their supply chain and to address these human right abuses. They demand companies and the government to free the child workers and introduce workers rights.

Solutions to stop the destruction of ecosystems and e-waste can be in many different ways, such as: using your mobile phone for a few more years if your phone can still function properly, Repairing your phone when its damaged, buying second hand phones, etc. These methods can reduce the pressure of landfills on the natural environment. Every phone that is used for a longer time means that it reduces the pressure on mines, factories and landfills, these can help not just the environment, but the people who are affected in the industry.
