# Historical development

Nowadays, most of us here have smartphones which we use every day, but it wasn't always the case so today we will talk about the history of the smartphone and its development. 
So, to start, the first smartphone ever made was in 1992 by IBM engineer Frank Canova. It could do things like checking stocks, news, sports, and weather. 
Since then, in 1994, they made the 1st public available phone called the Simon and improved their previous model so that it could make telephone calls, send and receive emails, and it had apps like a calendar, appointment scheduler, calculator, world clock, and notepad. 
It was also a touchscreen device, had a stylus and used lithium powered batteries like modern day smartphones. 
However, the Simon ended up having poor sales because of its big heavy size and poor battery life, but it still revolutionized the tech world and inspired others to integrate on it. 

The next big jump after the Simon was the Ericsson R380 in 1999 (before Sony's partnership) and the BlackBerry 5810 in 2002. These were one of the first phones that allowed people to access the big internet, opening up a whole lot more things to do and both of them used old style buttons on part of the display like Nokia and Motorola which were also booming around this time. 

One of the biggest revolutionary smartphone upgrades that most of us can agree on is the creation of the iPhone in 2007 which was announced by the original creator Steve Jobs. He even said to himself that "this is a day that I have been looking forward to for 2 and a half years," and that "today, Apple is going to reinvent the phone." 
In 2000, Steve Jobs envisioned the iPhone having a full touchscreen display without the need for a stylus or extra buttons which is how most phones are nowadays. The stylus was commonly used back in the day with touchscreen phones but removing it could make the experience less annoying and save money. 
6 weeks after the iPhones release, the screen was replaced from plastic to glass after Steve Jobs was upset that his keys scratched the display while it was in his pocket. Every phone nowadays followed by using glass and avoiding other materials that would be worse for the display. 
The Appstore was also added 1 year after its release in an update which made the iPhone even more appealing. 

The iPhone went on to be a huge success with over 6.1 million units being sold before being discontinued in July of 2008. 

This led to Apple helping to innovate other companies like Samsung to make their first phone, and they would go on to be competitive rivals in the smartphone market. 

The first Samsung Galaxy phone was announced and released in 2010. At the time, the Samsung galaxy S1 had the fastest graphical processing of any smartphone and was the thinnest smartphone at 9.9 mm. After being discontinued in 2012, the Samsung galaxy S1 ended up selling over 25 million units. Part of its success at the time was because their operating system from Google, android was blowing up with more than 500 million android devices being used at the time and it's open source so you can download whatever you want for free. It's still the most used smartphone operating system to this day. 

The global smartphone audience surpassed 1 billion in 2012 and 1.75 billion in 2014. Nowadays it is estimated to be over 5 billion, which is more than half of the world population. 

Smartphones have come a long way, and they are much bigger than before so much so that there are now double and triple foldable phones. 
Styluses and old style flip phones are also still a thing nowadays with it being evolved to have multiple screens. 
Some smartphones are even more powerful than PC's or laptops, but they still lack extra functionality.

# Societal Viewpoint on phones

Since the iPhone's launch, smartphones have brought us many benefits such as better communication and connectivity, apps like Instagram and snapchat have allowed us to connect and make friends with people across the globe, some may argue they have reduced isolation, many people have made connections through online communities such as games or forums bonding through shared interests with around 33% to 35% of young adults and teenagers meeting their online friends in real life, face-to-face according to articles from RTE and BBC. Something positive that unfortunately comes with many negatives. 

Studies from Oregon State University have addressed the question "Can these virtual relationships do for us what real-life relationships do?" with the sad answer being no and are said to even make things worse. The reason for why people who interact online feel lonely is because of the lack of meaningful connections that online interactions provide us with. They lack depth and the nuances of nonverbal cues which makes it very difficult to understand the meaning and emotions behind a message, resulting in misunderstandings. This absence of emotional bonds and is what has caused many people to still feel lonely despite having perhaps hundreds of online friends, it's their need for a real human connection that causes them to feel this way.

# Scientific Viewpoint Analysis
## Definining the scientific viewpoint
- From a scientific viewpoint the evolution of mobile phones to smartphones can be evaluated through the development of novel technologies, modification/adoption of pre-existing technologies, removal/replacement of redundant technologies and differences in the function of phones. Hence the distinction between mobile and smartphones can be illustrated through these differences.

### Differences in the functions of Phones
- Historically mobile phones could be distinguished by their rudimentary functions such as the ability to call, text and or play very simple games as such these phones may be known as feature phones. While there is no strict definition of a smartphone compared to feature phones, most distinctions are drawn when the hardware or Operating System (OS) within the phone can handle additional functionality besides the rudimentary functions of feature phones. Such definitions include the vague ability to run programs or applications besides said rudimentary features or the inclusion of networking hardware and sensors.

- These range to tighter definitions such as the ability to download and install third party applications, although such definitions would even exclude the first iPhone as a smartphone. iPhones only received this functionality in the second generation of iPhones. Despite the lack of clear a distinction, smartphones have distinguished themselves through the additional functionality they provide, hence their role has also shifted from simple communication devices becoming personal data storage devices, multimedia devices and in essence portable computers trading power for a smaller form factor. Notably this intent to create a phone with the capabilities of a home computer was held within the design philosophy of one of the first smartphones, the aformentioned IBM Simon.

### Development of novel technologies
1. Predictive text was a new OS feature introduced by the IBM Simon, unlike modern predictive text features the IBM Simon's PredictaKey Keyboard was a series of 6 letters which were displayed depending on what the phone predicted was the most likely to be used next. If the user was unsatisfied with the presented letters the 'other' button my be pressed to display the next set of likely 6 letters.
  
### Modification/adoption of previous technologies
1. Touch-screen is a preexisting technology, a touchscreen is defined as the ability for a surface area to simultaneously display an image or act as a 'screen' and receive an input. There are 2 forms of touch-screen, resistive touch-screen and capacitive touch-screen. Resistive touch-screen used pressure from a physical object to generate an input signal this was regularly done with a stylus as seen with the original IBM Simon touchscreen and stylus. Capacitive touch-screen identified changes in the electro-static field by physical interaction from an electrically conductive object.

- Most smartphones used resistive touch-screens prior to the first generation iPhone, the first generation iPhone popularized the use of capacitive touchscreens and developed touchscreen technology by overcoming sensitivity, gesture recognition and smooth glass interface limitations. The adoption of touch-screen required a new development in OS design and the expanse of UI/UX areas of research in science for interfaces expecting human touch within the small display dimensions of mobile phones.
  
2. Operating systems (OS) to run computer devices are a preexisting technology even on feature phones. While feature phones often ran a heavily limited OS, smartphones adopted an OS closer to a desktop style OS allowing for third party applications to be downloaded and ran on the OS and allowing for greater functionality especially when connected to networks such as the internet. 

### Removal/replacement of redundant technologies
1. Buttons were the primary input method for feature phones which were largely dropped in favor for the previously mentioned resistive and capacitive touchscreens. While most buttons were removed some physical buttons were still present such a navigation buttons on the bottom and media controls on the side for controlling volume or allowing quick switching on or off of the device.

# Ethical -
## Exploitation of workers
One ethical problem with mobile phones is where they come from. Parts of mobile phones contain rare earth materials that only come from countries like the Democratic Republic of Congo(DRC). Workers mine for cobalt and are often exploited. For example, a Congolese boy named Paul was exploited to work in the mines for a day straight, using him for child labour. He started mining when he was 12 and worked with little to no safety equipment and little pay. He was constantly ill and sick while underground. This affects his physical health, the harmful dust he breathed while working will affect his health in the long term. Paul was also at risk of the mines collapsing on him, as the mines are dark, tight spaces and are always prone to collapsing. Around 80 miners have died underground during 2014-2015.

There is no coporate shame on the companies buying these materials. The material is transported from the mines to large markets and then bought by companies. The three top buyers for cobalt are Ningbo Shanshan Tianjin Bamo from China and L&F Materials from South Korea, where they reportedly bought $90 million worth of cobalt in 2013. This material is then exported to different countries like China and Korea. Where it is then used to produce batteries for electric devices like smartphones or vehicles like EVs. 

## Environmental impact
Another ethical problem is the carbon footprint of smartphones. In 2022, smartphones produced 146 millions tons of carbon dioxide emissions from manufacturing and shipping. 
The factories where smartphones are manufactured uses a lot of energy, which is mostly made from fossil fuels, increasing greenhouse gas emissions. Most of the smartphone's carbon footprint comes from the manufacturing phase, where it is 70% to 85%
Cobalt has been historically mined for years, meaning that the mining has caused ecological damage to the local environments. The trees and other greenery that was once there cannot take in the carbon and turn into oxygen. The ecological damage has also destroyed natural habitats, meaning that the local wildlife are also displaced.

Throwing away smartphones can cause fatal damage to the ecosystems, plants, animals and people living there. They end up in landfills every year through illegal dumping and other ways, this is known as e-waste. 
Smartphone batteries contain harmful materials like mercury and lead. When it is dumped, it can leak and pollute different water sources which is deadly, as fish can consume this material, infecting it, and be spread to humans through consumption.

## Solutions
To combat the exploitation of workers in Congo, support organisations like Amnesty International, where they fight for exploited workers and human rights. Currently, Amnesty are calling out multinational companies that buy these rare earth materials to investigate their supply chain and to address these human right abuses. They demand companies and the government to free the child workers and introduce workers rights.

Solutions to stop the destruction of ecosystems and e-waste can be in many different ways, such as: using your mobile phone for a few more years if your phone can still function properly, Repairing your phone when its damaged, buying second hand phones, etc. These methods can reduce the pressure of landfills on the natural environment. Every phone that is used for a longer time means that it reduces the pressure on mines, factories and landfills, these can help not just the environment, but the people who are affected in the industry.
